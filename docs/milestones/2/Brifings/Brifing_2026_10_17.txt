RNDTransit features
1. Map view — clarity and confidence

Feature: The app displays the trip’s points and route on a map. Route generation considers elevation and known restricted areas, while the map helps users understand and follow the route.

User benefits:

- Clearer navigation: Users can see their position, destination, and route, reducing confusion and missed turns.

- Better preparation: Elevation information helps users anticipate climbs and judge whether a trip suits their abilities.

- Support for safer decisions: Accounting for known restricted areas helps users avoid unsuitable routes. Safety still depends on accurate information and actual conditions.

- Confidence to explore: Understanding the route can make unfamiliar places feel more approachable.

The deeper benefit is freedom to discover new places while maintaining a sense of control. Finding new places is a benefit of the overall trip experience; the map supports it by reducing uncertainty.

Jimmy’s revised 5 Whys:

1. Why show the trip on a map?

   So users understand where they are and where to go next.

2. Why does that matter?

   It helps them avoid wrong turns and unsuitable areas.

3. Why avoid those problems?

   They can interrupt the trip and create unnecessary stress.

4. Why reduce that stress?

   Users can feel more confident exploring unfamiliar surroundings.

5. Why is that confidence valuable?

   It lets users enjoy discovery while feeling prepared and in control.

2. Compass view — curiosity and discovery

Feature: The app provides compass-style guidance toward the next destination, revealing less route detail than the map view.

“AI implementation” needs a separate explanation: a compass interface does not inherently require AI. If AI chooses destinations or adapts guidance, describe that specific capability.

User benefits:

- Anticipation: Revealing less information can make the next destination feel like a discovery.

- A playful experience: Following directional clues can turn navigation into part of the activity.

- More personal exploration: Users may feel that they are uncovering a place themselves rather than following a fully revealed itinerary.

- Memorable moments: Unexpected discoveries can give users stories and experiences to share afterward.

The deeper benefit is the pleasure of curiosity and discovery. Surprise alone is not necessarily positive: users need enough guidance to feel comfortable. Access to the map and essential safety information would help preserve that balance.

Caio’s revised 5 Whys:

1. Why offer compass navigation?

   To let users approach destinations without revealing every detail beforehand.

2. Why reveal less detail?

   It creates anticipation about what they will discover.

3. Why create anticipation?

   Curiosity can make the journey itself enjoyable.

4. Why make the journey enjoyable?

   Users participate more actively in exploring their surroundings.

5. Why is that valuable?

   The trip can feel like a personal adventure, creating a stronger sense of discovery.

3. Multiplayer — connection and cooperation

Feature: Users can create group trips with individual and shared tasks, receive trip-specific roles, and view members’ locations. The app monitors whether members remain within a specified radius.

User benefits:

- Shared enjoyment: Friends can experience discoveries and complete activities together.

- Meaningful participation: Roles give each person a purpose and an opportunity to contribute.

- Easier coordination: Location awareness helps members understand where the group is and recognize separation.

- More possibilities: Sharing effort, equipment, and responsibilities can make some trips more practical.

- Social connection: Working toward a shared goal creates opportunities for conversation, cooperation, and shared memories.

The deeper benefit is belonging through a shared experience. Tasks and roles are valuable when they improve cooperation and enjoyment; adding gamification simply because other apps use it does not explain a user benefit. Location tracking also supports awareness but cannot guarantee group safety.
